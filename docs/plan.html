Word Ladder

An installable web game. Change one letter at a time to turn the start word into the end word. Play solo, or team up online with a friend anywhere: you add a word, they add the next, and you climb the ladder together.

What you asked for

  • A website that installs to the home screen and launches directly.
  • Single-player and two-player modes. In two-player, turns alternate: mine, then theirs.
  • Two-player is online, so friends in other cities can play on their own phones.
  • Two-player is a team game: you both work on the same ladder and win or lose together. No competing against each other.
  • Configurable word length (4, 5 or 6) and a custom start and end word.
  • The game suggests a start and end word that fit the chosen options.
  • Python for the tech stack.

How the modes play

Solo

Beat par

Each puzzle has a par: the shortest possible ladder length. You score stars by how close you get to par. Hints cost a star.

2 players · online

Team ladder

One shared ladder. You add a word, then your friend adds the next, and so on. You win together when the ladder reaches the end word. The team earns stars for finishing close to par.

Team scoring

Finished inTeam result
par (the shortest possible)★★★ Perfect ladder
par + 1 or 2★★

gave up togetherShows the optimal path, no stars

The result screen also shows who played which word, so you can see each person's contribution without turning it into a competition. Your team keeps a shared history: games played, average stars, and a streak of games finished.

Two ways to play a team game

Live

Both of you have the game open. Moves appear on the other phone instantly over a WebSocket. An optional 30 or 60 second turn timer keeps it moving.

Take your time

Best across time zones. Play your word, close the app, and your friend gets a notification. Each turn has a 24-hour deadline, so a game can't stall forever. You can have several games going at once.

The same game works both ways. If both players are online it feels live. If not, it falls back to notifications. No mode switch is needed.

Team rules in detail

  • A move must change exactly one letter and form a real word not already on the ladder.
  • An invalid word shows the reason (“not a word”, “changed 2 letters”) and the player can retry. Wrong guesses don't cost the team anything.
  • After each move, the game shows “closer”, “same distance” or “further”. This is measured by the real shortest path to the end word, which the server computes.
  • Undo: the team can step back one rung if a word led into a dead end. Each undo adds one step to the final count.
  • Hints: the team shares 2 hints per game. A hint shows which letter position to change next.
  • If a player misses the 24-hour turn deadline, the partner gets a reminder they can forward. The game waits; it never ends on its own.
  • The server checks every move, so the ladder is always the same on both phones.

Screen designs

Phone-first. Scroll sideways for all six screens. Orange is Player 1 and blue is Player 2 everywhere, so whose turn it is reads at a glance.

Word data and suggestions

Two word lists per length

  • Valid words: a broad list (e.g. ENABLE or SCOWL) used to accept moves. Lets players use less common words.
  • Common words: the top few thousand by frequency, used only for suggested start and end words, so suggestions are always words people recognise.
  • An optional blocklist filters out offensive words.

How a suggestion is made

  • At startup, build a graph per length: words are nodes, and words one letter apart are linked. Bucketing by wildcard pattern (c_ld) makes this fast.
  • Pick a random common word, run BFS, and choose an end word at the target distance.
  • The par (shortest path) is stored with the puzzle for scoring and hints.

Milestones

  1. Word engine. Load lists, build graphs, BFS, suggest, check, with pytest coverage.
  2. Backend core. Database tables, guest players, create/join game by invite code, alternating turns, server-side move checking, undo and team stars.
  3. Game UI. Home, setup, invite, game, waiting and result screens. Solo works end to end; online works by refreshing.
  4. Live play. WebSockets for instant moves and the "online" dot. Reconnects cleanly after the phone sleeps.
  5. Installable + notifications. Manifest, icons, service worker, web push "your turn" alerts, reminders.
  6. Deploy. Render or Fly.io with Postgres, then a real test with a friend in another city.
  7. Polish. Animations, sounds, team history and streaks, a daily team ladder.

Decisions for you

Live, take-your-time, or both? Recommended: both, as described above. Live-only is simpler (no push, no deadlines) but both players must be free at the same time.
Can a team have more than 2 people? The plan is built for 2. Turns rotating through 3 or 4 friends is a small change if you want it.
Guest players or accounts? Guests (name + cookie) are the fastest for friends to join. The catch: clearing the browser or switching phones loses your games. Email login can be added later to fix that.
Keep same-phone play too? It's cheap to add on top, for when you're sitting together.
Domain name? A free onrender.com address works. A custom domain looks nicer in invite links and costs about $10 a year.
